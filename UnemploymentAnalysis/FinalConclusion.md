Findings and Policy Insights: Unemployment in India, May 2019 to June 2020

This project analysed monthly unemployment data of Indian states from May 2019 to June 2020. The findings are grouped under the three objectives
of the task.

Impact of Covid-19 on unemployment rates

1.1 Average unemployment rose from about 9-10% to nearly 25% after the lockdown
Before the lockdown, the average unemployment rate stayed around 9-10%. In April 2020 it jumped to about 23.6% and in May 2020 it reached about
24.8%, which is more than double the earlier level.
[Chart: unemployment trend over time]

1.2 By June 2020 the rate fell to about 11.9%, but stayed above the pre-lockdown level
The rate dropped sharply from the peak but remained higher than the 9-10% seen before the lockdown. This shows that the recovery was not 
complete.

1.3 Puducherry had the biggest rise after the lockdown (about 37 percentage points)
Puducherry is followed by Tamil Nadu (about 23), Jharkhand (about 22) and Bihar (about 18). The lockdown therefore did not affect all states
equally.
[Chart: top 10 states by increase after lockdown]

Key patterns and seasonal trends in the data

2.1 Tripura (about 28%) and Haryana (about 26%) had the highest average unemployment over the whole period
Tripura is not among the states with the biggest increase after the lockdown, which suggests its unemployment was already high before Covid. 
Haryana, Jharkhand and Bihar appear in both state charts, so they combine high overall unemployment with a large increase after the lockdown.
[Chart: top 10 states by average unemployment rate]

2.2 Urban areas averaged 13.17% unemployment compared with 10.32% in rural areas
Urban unemployment was about 2.85 percentage points higher than rural unemployment over the whole period.
[Chart: urban vs rural]

2.3 April (about 23.6%) and May (about 16.7%) are high because of the lockdown, not the season
The monthly averages for the other months stay at around 9-10%. The data covers only about 14 months, so real seasonal trends cannot be 
confirmed.
[Chart: monthly pattern]

Insights for economic and social policy

3.1 Emergency support should be ready for sudden shocks. A rise from about 10% to nearly 25% in two months shows that cash transfers and job 
schemes must be quick to activate.

3.2 Relief should be targeted at the hardest-hit states. Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, Jharkhand and Bihar saw the largest increases and need priority
support.

3.3 States with long-standing unemployment need long-term job creation. Tripura and Haryana had high unemployment across the whole period,
which points to a structural problem and not just a Covid effect.

3.4 Urban workers need more protection. Urban unemployment was higher than rural. Rural areas have employment guarantee schemes, while urban
informal workers have less support.

3.5 Support should continue after reopening. Unemployment stayed above the pre-lockdown level in June 2020, so small businesses need credit
and wage support until recovery is complete.


Overall Takeaway

The Covid-19 lockdown caused a sharp rise in unemployment in India, but the effect was not the same everywhere. It depended on the state and 
on whether the area was urban or rural. Quick support is needed during sudden shocks, and long-term job creation is needed where unemployment 
is chronically high.
